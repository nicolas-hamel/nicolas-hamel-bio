Bring process, utilities, cold chain, energy, Historian and production-performance data into one governed operational context without tying the plant to one equipment manufacturer.

Executive summary

Agriculture value chains and agri-food plants often depend on equipment from several manufacturers, strict temperature or process windows, significant utility consumption and production records held in separate systems. A vendor-independent OT/IT data platform can connect these domains around one operating model while existing PLCs, quality systems and specialist equipment remain authoritative.

The practical objective: give operations, maintenance, energy and production teams consistent data and faster context, from a process deviation or refrigeration alarm to its utility, batch, quality or downtime consequence.

CODRA documents Panorama in process industries and agri-food environments, including SCADA, Historian and MES applications. Published examples include the Akiolis group's animal by-product processing operations.

Operational domains to connect

Process

Lines, vessels and packaging

Authorised real-time status, alarms, setpoints and production context from PLC-controlled processes, filling, packaging and material handling.

Cold chain

Refrigeration and storage conditions

Refrigeration plant, cold rooms, temperatures, defrost cycles, doors, power status and alarm history, with clear ownership of food-safety records.

Utilities

Energy, water and wastewater

Electrical distribution, boilers, compressed air, steam, water, effluent, generators and meters linked to production state and shift context.

Facilities and security

BMS, access and technical alarms

Building services, fire information, access control and selected security events integrated where they improve site operations and incident response.

From real-time control to production context

Panorama E2 can support the SCADA and HMI layer. Panorama H2 provides collection, archiving and analysis of operational history. Panorama COOX provides MES capabilities, with current positioning around QPI and production-performance analysis. These components can share context without confusing their responsibilities.

  • SCADA: real-time supervision, alarms, commands and operator interfaces.
  • Historian: reliable time-series data for event reconstruction, comparison and investigation.
  • EMS: utility baselines, consumption intensity and performance linked to production conditions.
  • MES: governed production, QPI, OEE, downtime and operator context according to the licensed scope.
  • Enterprise exchange: controlled interfaces with quality, maintenance, planning or reporting systems.

The project should define the system of record for batch, traceability and quality data. A shared platform improves context; it does not remove the need for validated records and accountable processes.

Multi-site operations and local autonomy

Processors may operate plants, cold stores, collection sites or utilities across several locations. Local SCADA applications should continue through a WAN outage. Central services can then consolidate selected alarms, history, engineering standards and performance comparisons, with store-and-forward and clear recovery rules.

This pattern supports standardisation without forcing every plant into one central failure domain. It also allows phased modernisation: begin with one constrained process or utility, prove the data and workflow, then reuse the governed model elsewhere.

Agri-food buyer checklist

  1. Define one decision to improve: process deviation, refrigeration failure, energy intensity, water loss, downtime or production performance.
  2. Map PLCs, instrumentation, protocols, existing HMIs, meters, refrigeration controls, Historian, MES and quality records.
  3. Set data-quality rules: timestamps, units, product or batch context, operating phases and handling of missing data.
  4. Define local autonomy, safety-system authority, WAN-loss behaviour, buffering and recovery.
  5. Agree systems of record and interfaces for traceability, quality, maintenance and enterprise reporting.
  6. Prove a representative workflow and lifecycle support model before scaling across lines or plants.

Frequently asked questions

What can an agri-food Unified Control Centre combine?

It can combine authorised process, utility, refrigeration, cold-room, energy, water, wastewater, building, security, Historian and production-performance data. The authoritative PLC, safety system, quality record and MES responsibility must remain clear.

Does Panorama Suite replace existing production PLCs?

No. It can supervise and contextualise heterogeneous controllers and systems through validated protocols and gateways. Existing PLCs and safety systems remain where they are technically appropriate and authoritative.

Can the same platform support SCADA, Historian and MES use cases?

Yes. Panorama E2 supports real-time applications, Panorama H2 provides Historian capabilities and Panorama COOX provides MES functions. The required modules, interfaces and licensed scope must be confirmed for each project.

Can several plants operate locally while reporting centrally?

Yes. A distributed architecture can preserve local process supervision and control while centralising selected history, alarms, administration and performance reporting. Store-and-forward, data quality and recovery rules must be designed.
