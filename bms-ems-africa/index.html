Executive summary

Panorama Suite can provide a shared operational data layer for Building Management System (BMS) and Energy Management System (EMS) applications. A BMS concentrates on the real-time operation of building services. An EMS adds the longer-term measurement, comparison and improvement of energy and utility performance. They overlap, but they are not identical.

The practical objective: connect HVAC, electrical distribution, lighting, water, lifts, meters and selected safety or security information around one operational model, while keeping appropriate local control and specialist subsystems in place.

CODRA describes Panorama as able to collect and analyse information from lighting, water, energy, LV/HV networks, lifts and access control. Its H2 Energy offer adds utility-focused collection, historisation and analysis. See CODRA’s SCADA System in Building sector – BMS/FM and Boost your drive to energy efficiency with H2 Energy.

Why building and energy operations become fragmented

Large buildings and estates rarely begin with one coherent architecture. They accumulate HVAC controllers, electrical meters, generators, UPS systems, access control, fire panels, lifts, water systems and specialist applications from different generations and manufacturers. Each subsystem may work, while the operating team still has no common view of alarms, consumption or site performance.

The cost of fragmentation appears in daily operations: repeated alarms, manual consolidation, inconsistent naming, slow diagnosis and upgrades that force a decision about one supplier’s wider ecosystem. A vendor-independent platform creates an integration layer above that diversity. It does not require every field system to be replaced.

One platform for BMS and EMS applications

BMS application

Operate the building in real time

Consolidate status, alarms, commands and visualisation for HVAC, electrical services, water, lighting, lifts and other technical systems according to each operator’s role.

EMS application

Understand and improve utility performance

Historise electricity, water, gas, steam or compressed-air data; compare periods and sites; and give energy teams evidence for investigation and improvement.

Panorama Suite supports standard and specialist communications, but every project must validate the exact protocols, device profiles, drivers and gateways required by the installed base. Data quality, metering boundaries and naming conventions matter as much as connectivity.

Multi-site operations without losing local autonomy

Retail portfolios, banks, campuses, hospitals, airports and data-centre groups often need a central view without making every site dependent on a permanent WAN connection. An Edge-to-Service architecture can keep essential BMS operation local while centralising selected alarms, energy data, administration and fleet-level comparisons.

The design should specify what remains available during a network outage, which commands may be issued centrally, how data is buffered and synchronised, and who owns the application after handover. CODRA documents this distributed pattern in Panorama Edge-to-Service.

Operational and energy use cases

  • Data centres: correlate cooling, electrical capacity, UPS, generators, environmental conditions and site alarms.
  • Commercial and public estates: compare utility performance across branches, offices, schools, hospitals or government buildings.
  • Transport and campuses: coordinate distributed technical systems while maintaining site-level operation.
  • Industrial facilities: connect building utilities with production context without merging every control responsibility.

Automatic Fault Detection (AFD) can support investigation by learning normal historical behaviour and alerting teams to abnormal patterns. It is decision support, not a substitute for instrumentation, engineering limits or maintenance procedures. See CODRA’s AFD: detect the abnormal before it becomes an incident.

How to evaluate a BMS and EMS project

  1. Define the operating decisions: identify the alarms, commands, comparisons and reports that teams must act on.
  2. Map the installed base: controllers, meters, protocols, networks, existing BMS applications and data ownership.
  3. Set the energy boundaries: utilities, tariff periods, baselines, submeters, production or occupancy context and data-quality rules.
  4. Design resilience: local autonomy, WAN loss, redundancy, buffering, backup and recovery.
  5. Prove a representative pilot: include at least one difficult subsystem, a usable operator workflow and an auditable energy use case.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a BMS and an EMS?

A BMS focuses primarily on the real-time operation of building services. An EMS focuses on measuring, analysing and improving energy and utility performance. A shared OT/IT data platform can support both while keeping their responsibilities clear.

Does Panorama Suite replace every building controller?

No. It can integrate and coordinate heterogeneous systems while existing controllers and specialist applications remain where they are appropriate. Each interface still needs to be validated.

Can one BMS and EMS cover multiple sites?

Yes. A distributed architecture can preserve essential local operation while providing central visibility, administration and performance comparison across sites. Connectivity-loss behaviour must be designed explicitly.

Is a BMS application the same as SCADA?

BMS and SCADA overlap in real-time supervision and control, but they describe different application contexts. On this website, both are applications enabled by the broader Panorama Suite OT/IT Data Management Platform.

Can AFD guarantee energy savings?

No. Automatic Fault Detection can identify abnormal behaviour earlier and support investigation. Savings depend on instrumentation, data quality, operating conditions and the organisation’s ability to act on findings.
