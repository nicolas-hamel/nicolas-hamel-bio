Executive summary

Central African projects often combine dispersed assets, mixed equipment, multilingual teams and variable access to specialist support. Panorama Suite provides a vendor-independent OT/IT Data Management Platform for Unified Control Centres without requiring the operator to standardise every controller, subsystem or field device on one manufacturer.

Country-level scoping may begin in Cameroon, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the Republic of the Congo, Gabon or another Central African market. The integration and lifecycle-support model is then defined for the country, sector and installed base.

Regional engagement route: customers can contact CODRA's Johannesburg office directly. CODRA can support the initial qualification and help identify a suitable certified integration partner in the country or a neighbouring market.

This separates product ownership from project integration. CODRA develops and supports Panorama Suite, while the selected System Integrator Partner provides the project engineering and local delivery capabilities agreed for the application.

A platform for a Unified Control Centre, not one isolated application

Panorama Suite is an OT/IT Data Management Platform for Unified Control Centres. SCADA is one application supported by the platform, alongside GeoSCADA, BMS, EMS, Historian, PSIM and MES. These applications can share a common operational data and engineering foundation.

This approach is particularly relevant where existing PLCs, RTUs, meters, building controllers, security subsystems and specialist applications must remain in service. The platform creates a consistent operational layer while preserving equipment choice and avoiding dependence on one automation manufacturer.

Applications and sectors in the Central African context

The first application should follow the operational outcome. Relevant routes include:

  • Energy, utilities and water: SCADA, GeoSCADA, EMS, alarm management, historical analysis and multi-site operations;
  • Mining and process industries: operational visibility, production context, historian and MES applications across mixed installed bases;
  • Buildings and infrastructure: BMS, EMS and resource-performance applications across individual or distributed facilities;
  • Security and transport: PSIM and Unified Control Centres combining operational and physical-security information;
  • Distributed public infrastructure: autonomous local operation with central visibility, reporting and controlled administration.

Every project still requires an installed-base assessment, protocol validation, cybersecurity boundaries and a clear division of engineering, commissioning, support and lifecycle responsibilities.

Design the delivery model with the architecture

The system architecture and partner model should be defined together. Remote assets, connectivity constraints and limited access to specialist skills can materially affect support, recovery and upgrade planning.

Maintainability

Build local capability into the project

Define training, documentation, engineering ownership, version management and first-line support before commissioning.

Connectivity

Keep essential operations autonomous

Confirm what must continue during WAN or central-service loss and how data is reconciled when communications return.

Partner responsibilities

Make ownership explicit

Agree what CODRA, the certified integrator and the customer own across architecture, engineering, testing, cybersecurity, support and escalation.

Multi-site design

Central visibility without central fragility

Use Edge-to-Service patterns where local applications need autonomy alongside central hypervision, administration and reporting.

How to start a Central African programme

  1. Contact the Johannesburg office: outline the country, operating need, project stage and required local capabilities.
  2. Map the installed base: equipment families, protocols, current applications, data quality, networks and lifecycle constraints.
  3. Define the application boundary: identify the first SCADA, GeoSCADA, BMS, EMS, Historian, PSIM, MES or Unified Control Centre scope.
  4. Select the delivery model: agree whether the project needs an in-country certified integrator or support from a suitable partner in a neighbouring market.
  5. Validate a focused use case: confirm interoperability, operator usability, resilience and support processes before scaling.

Frequently asked questions

How can a Central African operator engage CODRA?

Operators can engage CODRA directly through its Africa business and technical office in Johannesburg. CODRA can then help organise the appropriate commercial, technical and integration route for the country and project.

Who delivers Panorama Suite projects in Central Africa?

Projects can be delivered with a suitable CODRA-certified System Integrator Partner in the customer country or, where appropriate, a neighbouring country. Engineering, commissioning, support and escalation responsibilities are defined for each project.

Is Panorama Suite only a SCADA product?

No. Panorama Suite is an OT/IT Data Management Platform for Unified Control Centres. SCADA is one application supported by the platform, alongside GeoSCADA, BMS, EMS, Historian, PSIM and MES.

Does a Central African Panorama Suite architecture require permanent cloud connectivity?

No. Essential applications can remain local and autonomous. Central or cloud services can be added selectively when connectivity, operating requirements and the cybersecurity assessment justify them.
