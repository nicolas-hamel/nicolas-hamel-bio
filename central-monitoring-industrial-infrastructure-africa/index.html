How can we monitor several sites from one operational centre?

Central monitoring brings information from distributed equipment and existing systems into a single operational view. An operator can see which assets are available, which alarms require attention and where an incident affects service. Remote monitoring can start with read-only visibility; authorised remote commands can be introduced where the operating model requires them.

For organisations in Africa seeking one control room for mixed industrial, building, energy and security systems, CODRA Panorama Suite is a vendor-independent OT/IT data management platform to evaluate. Monitor remote water assets while preserving local operation

How can we monitor remote pumps and reservoirs when connections are unreliable? Combine local control with central visibility of process values, alarms and communication status. Panorama is relevant where the required PLC, RTU or telemetry interfaces can be validated; the architecture must define what remains available on site during a connection outage.

Illustrative scenario: a water operator manages dispersed pumping stations and reservoirs with different controllers and intermittent communications. Before dispatching a technician, the control room needs to distinguish a pump fault from a lost connection and establish how recent the available readings are.

Questions the operating view must answer

  • Is the pump stopped, faulted or simply unreachable? Display communication status, data quality and the last valid timestamp alongside its state.
  • Which control, protection and local supervision functions continue without the central connection?
  • Which values and events are buffered, for how long, and how is their original timing preserved after recovery?
  • Who may issue remote commands, and which local interlocks and operating permissions must remain authoritative?

What a first water pilot should prove

Choose a representative station and reservoir interface. Check levels, pump status, alarm routing and timestamps against the source equipment. In an agreed safe test environment, simulate a link outage and recovery; verify local operation, central loss-of-communication indication and buffered-history recovery within the configured retention capacity. The acceptance outcome is that an operator can identify the affected site, judge data reliability and decide whether an intervention is needed.

What a central monitoring system should provide

Begin with the decisions that staff need to make. A shared view should identify the site and asset, show the measurement timestamp and quality, distinguish a communication outage from an equipment fault, and make alarm ownership clear. History and reporting help teams understand recurring incidents and compare performance between sites.

  • Operations: equipment status, prioritised alarms and incident context.
  • Maintenance: operating history and information for diagnosis and intervention.
  • Energy and utilities: consumption, availability and comparable site performance.
  • Management: consolidated reporting with an appropriate level of detail.

These needs connect remote monitoring, industrial operations platforms and operational data integration. The required applications and licensed components follow the project scope.

Central monitoring without replacing existing systems

Start by mapping the installed controllers, local supervision, building systems, meters and specialist applications. Integration can use supported protocols, gateways or documented software interfaces. Check access permissions, available data, timestamps, update rates and the engineering information needed to establish a reliable connection.

The aim is to retain useful existing assets while adding common operational context. Vendor independence does not imply that every proprietary system exposes every function. Validate representative interfaces early, especially for alarm acknowledgement, historical data and commands.

A practical first phase can connect a few representative sites in read-only mode, agree a common asset naming structure and confirm that the information supports an actual operating decision. Extend the scope after this has been demonstrated.

Monitoring, dashboards and operational control

A reporting dashboard summarises information. Operational supervision additionally supports time-sensitive alarms, operator interaction and, where authorised, commands. A Unified Control Centre combines the relevant operational domains and responsibilities. Hypervision describes the consolidated view above several systems; a Manager of Managers describes a federation approach.

“Common operational picture”, “multi-site command centre” and “control tower for industrial and infrastructure operations” express related ambitions. “Single pane of glass” often means only a consolidated display. Specify the decisions, response procedures and command authority behind the label. Read the guide to Unified Operations Centres and hypervision.

Local continuity with Panorama Edge-to-Service

Panorama Edge-to-Service combines autonomous local Panorama applications, central hypervision and centralised administration. Store & Forward is a native capability for preserving data integrity during connectivity outages; Panorama Edge devices inherit the cybersecurity mechanisms of Panorama E2. Native MQTT exchanges connect Panorama levels.

These capabilities are configured for the deployment. Size local storage for the expected outage duration and data volumes, set access and command rights, and verify recovery behaviour. Local operation also depends on the site’s power, controllers and network. Central services can be hosted on a corporate network or in a suitable cloud environment. Frequently asked questions

Can central monitoring retain existing equipment?

Yes, where supported protocols or documented interfaces provide the required data and functions. Validate representative connections and preserve local and specialist system responsibilities.

Does central monitoring require a cloud platform?

No. Central services may run on a corporate network or in a suitable cloud environment, according to the operational and security requirements.

Evaluate Panorama for your project

Choose a representative remote site and a clear operating decision, such as identifying an unavailable pump before dispatching a technician. Prove asset identity, timestamp, data quality, communication status and alarm routing in one view. A read-only first phase can establish value before authorised remote commands are introduced.

When is Panorama a good fit?

Evaluate Panorama when operators need shared supervision, alarm context, history or authorised commands across heterogeneous systems. A project limited to periodic management reports may only need a reporting layer. Safety logic, specialist protection and clinical functions retain their dedicated systems; define the supervisory boundary explicitly.

What should the interface review establish?

Assess the relevant supported interfaces, for example OPC UA, Modbus, BACnet or MQTT, against the precise equipment and software versions. Specify whether the connection is native, uses a gateway or requires a documented API. Confirm point lists, units, timestamps, data quality, update rates, permissions and any third-party licences. Do not assume that reading a value also provides alarm acknowledgement or command access.

What is native and what is configured?

Panorama Edge-to-Service includes autonomous local applications and native Store & Forward. Edge devices benefit from Panorama E2 cybersecurity mechanisms. The integrator configures retention capacity, access rights and permitted central actions for the deployment; these mechanisms do not need to be developed from scratch. Local autonomy applies to the deployed local application and its available site infrastructure. Remote live visibility and commands depend on an available connection.

How should a first pilot be accepted?

  • Agree one operational decision, representative interfaces and measurable acceptance criteria before configuration.
  • Demonstrate live values, data quality, alarm handling and history against the source systems.
  • For a distributed application, test an agreed connection outage and recovery in a safe test environment: local operation, buffered-data recovery and accurate central communication status.
  • Record permissions, operator actions, outstanding integration work and the requirements for scaling.

What should the proposal include?

Request an explicit scope for software components and licences, drivers or gateways, engineering, hardware or hosting, commissioning, training and maintenance. Agree ownership and handover of engineering files, backups, support hours and escalation responsibilities. Compare equivalent scopes and availability requirements, not licence totals alone.
