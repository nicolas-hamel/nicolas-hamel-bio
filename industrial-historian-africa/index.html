Executive summary

Panorama H2 is CODRA’s Historian offer for collecting, archiving and analysing operational data locally or in a hosted model. A useful industrial Historian does more than retain values. It preserves trustworthy timestamps and enough operating context to explain what equipment, alarms, teams and production conditions were involved.

The central question is not “How much data can we store?” It is “Can operators and engineers retrieve dependable evidence quickly enough to understand events, compare performance and improve decisions?”

See CODRA’s official Panorama H2 – Data Historian & Reporting page and its guide to Data Historian: Data Archiving & Time-Series Analysis.

A Historian is an operational evidence layer

Operational information arrives from PLCs, DCSs, RTUs, meters, machines, building systems, instruments, third-party software and manual inputs. The Historian must preserve chronology while making relationships visible: a process value before an alarm, a change of operating phase, the equipment in service, the team on shift and the time taken to acknowledge or correct an event.

This context distinguishes an operational Historian from a generic archive. It supports post-event analysis, performance comparison, compliance evidence, maintenance investigation and the data preparation needed for more advanced analytics.

Data quality and governance come before dashboards

  • Timestamp integrity: define source timestamps, time zones, clock synchronisation and store-and-forward behaviour.
  • Context: preserve asset, site, unit, batch, operating phase and alarm relationships.
  • Quality flags: distinguish measured, calculated, substituted, stale and unavailable values.
  • Retention: align raw and aggregated histories with operational, regulatory and cost requirements.
  • Ownership: define who may create tags, alter calculations, approve reports and export data.

A fast database cannot compensate for inconsistent naming, missing instrumentation or unclear responsibility. Those foundations should be included in the first pilot.

One history serving several operational applications

Local, central and multi-site Historian architecture

Remote and critical sites may need to continue collecting data when the WAN is unavailable. A distributed design can retain local acquisition and buffering while transferring selected history to a central service for group analysis. The project should define recovery order, duplicate handling, bandwidth limits, time synchronisation and the authority of central commands separately from data collection.

Panorama Edge-to-Service provides a reference pattern for local autonomy, central administration and controlled data transfer. See Panorama Edge-to-Service.

From historical data to earlier anomaly detection

Automatic Fault Detection (AFD) uses historical operating data to learn normal behaviour across different phases and flag abnormal patterns. The useful output is not an unexplained score; it is an alert that an engineer or operator can investigate with the underlying trends and context.

AFD does not repair poor sensors, missing context or inconsistent operating procedures. Its effectiveness depends on the Historian foundation and the organisation’s ability to review and act on findings. See AFD: detect the abnormal before it becomes an incident.

How to evaluate an industrial Historian project

  1. Choose representative decisions: incident analysis, energy comparison, maintenance investigation or production performance.
  2. Inventory sources and rates: tag volumes, changes, bursts, events, alarms, manual data and existing archives.
  3. Define context and quality: timestamps, asset hierarchy, calculations, quality flags and naming.
  4. Model retention and recovery: raw versus aggregated data, backup, restoration and disconnected operation.
  5. Test retrieval: prove that users can answer real questions within an acceptable time, not only that ingestion works.

Frequently asked questions

How is an industrial Historian different from a normal database?

An industrial Historian is designed around time-series acquisition, timestamps, quality, operational context and efficient retrieval of changing process data. A general database may still be used elsewhere in the architecture.

Can Panorama H2 collect data from an existing SCADA or BMS?

Yes, subject to the available interfaces and project design. The source protocols, data semantics, timestamps and ownership should be validated before migration or federation.

Does an industrial Historian have to be in the cloud?

No. CODRA describes Panorama H2 as available locally or in a hosted model. The correct choice depends on autonomy, cybersecurity, connectivity, governance and lifecycle requirements.

Can historical data support predictive maintenance?

Yes. Historical trends and anomaly detection can support earlier diagnosis and predictive-maintenance workflows, but outcomes depend on instrumentation, context, data quality and maintenance processes.

What should a Historian pilot prove?

It should prove reliable acquisition, timestamp and quality handling, recovery after disconnection, usable context, retrieval performance and at least one decision or report that users can validate.
